Comparing Apple Notes and QuickNote is a bit like comparing a filing cabinet to
a notepad in your pocket. They overlap — both hold text — but they're built for
different moments. Here's the honest breakdown, including the cases where Apple
Notes wins outright.

## Where Apple Notes wins

Apple Notes is a genuinely great app, and it's the right tool for a lot of jobs:

- **Sync everywhere.** iCloud keeps notes on your iPhone, iPad, and Mac. If
  cross-device access is the requirement, this is the answer.
- **Rich documents.** Scans, checklists, tables, attachments, shared folders —
  Notes handles real document work.
- **Long-term reference.** It's a fine home for things you'll want in five years.

## Where the friction is

The same features that make Notes powerful add weight at the exact moment you
need speed:

- **Capture is a journey, not a keystroke.** Open the app (or Spotlight, then
  Notes), create a note, deal with the full editor. On a slow day that's fine;
  mid-call, it's enough friction that thoughts get dropped.
- **Everything lands in your main library.** Your scratch thoughts live next to
  your important reference material, which quietly pollutes both.
- **You need an Apple ID in the loop.** Sync, storage, and search all assume the
  cloud. For notes you'd rather never leave your machine, that's a real
  consideration.

## Where QuickNote wins

QuickNote is deliberately the opposite trade:

- **One keystroke, anywhere.** ⌃⇧Space summons a slim panel over whatever you're
  doing; return saves and dismisses. The whole loop is under three seconds.
- **Local-only storage.** Notes live in a database on your Mac. No account, no
  sync service, no network access at all. Private by architecture, not by
  policy.
- **Structure optional.** The first line becomes the heading and the rest becomes
  the body — you never stop to format. Pin, search (⌘F), multi-select, merge,
  and drag-to-organize exist for when you're in organizing mode.

And to be clear about the trade in the other direction: **QuickNote does not
sync.** If you need a note on your iPhone tonight, capture it in Apple Notes.

## The system that actually works

Most people don't have a "notes app problem" — they have a capture problem. The
notes app is fine; the catching of thoughts before they evaporate is what's
broken. The setup that works:

1. **QuickNote is the inbox.** Every fleeting thought, meeting aside, idea at
   midnight, one keystroke and it's stored locally.
2. **A weekly five-minute sweep.** Open the inbox, pin what matters this week,
   copy the one or two keepers into Apple Notes if they're reference material or
   need to reach your phone.
3. **Everything else stays in QuickNote.** Most captures have done their job the
   moment you've read them once. Trash them guilt-free.

That's not "Apple Notes vs QuickNote" — it's Apple Notes *and* QuickNote, each
doing the job it's shaped for. Filing cabinet and pocket notepad.

## FAQ

**Is QuickNote free?** Yes — it's a free direct download for macOS 26 and later,
Apple silicon and Intel.

**Can QuickNote read my Apple Notes?** No. It's fully independent, with its own
local database and no integration with iCloud or Notes.app.

**Is my data safe if I delete the app?** Your notes live in QuickNote's local
store on your Mac; deleting the app doesn't chase them down, and there's no
cloud copy to delete for you.

**Does QuickNote work offline?** It never touches the network in the first
place — offline isn't a mode, it's the design.
